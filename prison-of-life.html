Bibhu Acharya

Prison of Life

‘Ages’ ago, there lived a simpleton; a boy, unaware of the devilish world outside, who was nothing but innocent and hence happy.

The word ‘ages’ isn’t just relative as I have forgotten when my mind was as innocent as it used to be long time ago. The dirty palms of Time have sculpted me into a cunning and wicked creature. However slow this transformation may be, seldom do I pay heed to the essence of it. As my tired and scared fingers entour this page today I vividly remember an incident from that glorious ‘age’ that I miss so much now a days.

I was ten. One of my uncles whom we call Tuna Mamu, used to visit us in our town. His humour and witty quickies always kept us rooted to our seats when he was around. One such quickie was:

“You are in a prison cell. There are two doors. One of them leads to death and the other to freedom. Each door is guarded by a security person of whom one always speaks the truth and the other, always lies. You are neither aware of the right path nor of the right person. You are given an option of asking only one question to any one of the guards to choose our path to success.”

As far as my memory can run back, it took me around a fortnight to figure out a rough answer. The accurate answer was a bit different though. The technical intricacy of the answer to this riddle is however irrelevant here. But the relevance is the essence of it. The answer goes as follows:

“You ask any one of the guys, ‘What would be the answer of that guy, if I asked him the right path to success?’, and you choose the other path.”

Now my crooked mind which, fortunately or unfortunately is more matured compared to that of ‘ages’ ago, tries to analyse every event that occurs to and for me. Putting this situation box, my mind is thrilled to take an analysis.

Like the prison, Life always gives us two options, the right path and the wrong path. Life is also kind enough to give us righteous friends and loving relatives who are like the truthful guard. We are also surrounded by selfish friendship.

Secondly the outside world is very harsh. For every step to success, there are thousands who try to pull you back. There will be people who like to climb on your shoulders to rise up. A successful person is one who has the glass to see through right or wrong.

Thank you Tuna Mamu for this early ember wisdom. Thank you Oh Thee for making me realise that it’s high time to carve this glass now.
