Believers & Atheists

One fine morning I opened my mail box and something, among other subject heads, caught my intriguing attention. There was a forwarded message that was sent by a friend. This had a wonderful link to a website to perform a small act of Pooja (prayer/ worship/offering) for the Deity of Knowledge Lord Ganesha. I liked it and forwarded it to all my friends who, I had thought, shared the same belief as that of mine; and would not mind to this small Pooja with the click of a mouse. Then feeling a heart at peace and a satisfied smile, I logged out. A few days passed. One fine morning, propelled by the daily habit of checking my mails, I opened the mailbox and was surprised to see the subject which was a reply to my email from one of my friends. Quite excited and anxious about comments and sarcasm, as all my friends expect from this friend in special-let’s call him Jerry, I opened the mail. I was totally dismayed at the one-liner comment attached. The comment was a question against the beliefs of millions of people. But as expected, along came a reply to his comments from another friend of ours that referred to the eloping of traditional and cultural heritage of India in the minds of youngsters, like my special friend Jerry. To this, Jerry snapped back the following sentences.

“People believe in different gods....different rituals.... just look back history.... all those blind beliefs are getting lesser and lesser.... most of the things which they did that time...we feel they were so stupid...why don’t u follow all that saying it’s your culture?? ... Why no human sacrifices?... sati... why stopped? What u r doing now in temples etc.. Will be looked upon by future generation in the same way!! "Just look around the world.... younger generation is getting better... there are millions of people living happily without any religion!.. After looking at the different groups, it's obvious that believing in god/temples/ churches/ mosques blah blah... doesn't make much sense... come on...Open your eyes... don’t just go after what was embedded in your mind when you were young... when u had no ability to think... what I write above.. I am sure all of u know... but all are afraid.... "Why take risk with own life.. Just follow"... concept!!...

-Jerry

This interesting mail forced me to write down the following article. But before I start, I needed to brush up with the statistical figures (as that is the best way to wrap a leather cap around the empty earthen pot). I clicked on the WORLD WIDE WEB and was astonished at the figures. Firstly I would like you to go through the statistics of the world religions by adherents below.

# Christianity: 2.1 billion # Islam: 1.5 billion # Secular/Nonreligious/Agnostic/Atheist: 1.1 billion # Hinduism: 900 million # Chinese traditional religion: 394 million # Buddhism: 376 million # primal-indigenous: 300 million # African Traditional & Diasporic: 100 million # Sikhism: 23 million # Juche: 19 million # Spiritism: 15 million # Judaism: 14 million # Baha'i: 7 million # Jainism: 4.2 million # Shinto: 4 million # Cao Dai: 4 million # Zoroastrianism: 2.6 million # Tenrikyo: 2 million # Neo-Paganism: 1 million # Unitarian-Universalism: 800 thousand # Rastafarianism: 600 thousand # Scientology: 500 thousand For the year 2000, David B. Barrett (Encyclopedia Britannica and World Christian Encyclopedia, 2001) classified 150,089,508 (2.5% of world's population) as atheists, and 768,158,954 people as Nonreligious" (12.7% of the world) for a total of 918,248,462 (15.2% of the world). These calculations by Barrett include all agnostics and others in our "Secular/Nonreligious/Agnostic/Atheist" category. Of the people in this grouping, it is estimated that 40 to 50% have a stated traditionally "theistic" belief in God, deities or a Higher Power. The highest figure we have for "Nonreligious" is 20% of the world population, or about 1.2 billion: "Over 20 percent of the world's population does not claim any allegiance to a religion. Most are agnostics. Others are atheists, who deny the existence of God." (O'Brien, Joanne & Martin Palmer. The State of Religion Atlas. Simon & Schuster: New York (1993). Pg 41.). Estimates for atheism alone (as a primary religious preference) range from 200 to 240 million. But these come primarily from China and former Soviet Union nations (especially Russia). Courtesy: World Wide Web

So does it mean that 5 billion people walking the face of earth are fools against 1.1 billion? Few days back I had come across a very interesting book, by internationally renowned author Paulo Coelho, Like the Flowing River. In the chapter "The Music coming from the Chapel", he writes,

...Accustomed as we are to the mountains and the fields, we know God is everywhere and that there is no need for us to go into a man-made building in order to find him...

Jerry, who happens to be from this so-called new generation and carries quite blatant views regarding our society and beliefs, is right that we have been carrying the age old etches in our unexplored mind of the blind beliefs. But here Jerry forgets that the pioneers of these beliefs were not fools. They were of much higher intellect a matured minded people who formulated these beliefs so as to keep a discipline and help people, with the help of these beliefs, not to erase the Divine inclination, or the realisation of the existence of The Supreme Almighty. But certain misinterpretations led to the growth of weeds – the weeds about which Jerry was referring to. Another thing that intrigues many of the non-Indians is the existence of so many Gods in Hindu religion. Every now and then I run into someone or other who keeps on asking me, ‘Do you really have 330,000 Deities in your religion?’ Firstly to them I always quote the marvellous definition of religion as rightly said by Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi or Bapu as we refer him;

...my religion is a private and personal matter between me and my God and no one has the right to interfere...

Secondly, Bhagvad Geeta gives what Lord Krishna says to Arjuna, ‘I am the only one and only, am I. God is one. So how can there be the concept of so many Gods in any religion? My boss at office once rightly put it in the following words when he was asked the same question during a party: All the many forms are only a way to perceive Him. We give Him different forms and names and images only to manifest our beliefs.

I really pity the Atheists or the so called Non-religious people as they call themselves, for they do not know that their very existence on this materialistic abode is for a reason which they must fulfil among all other duties. It’s not an experiment that they are living their lives. At some point in their lives, they do realise that the voice of their heart is speaking to them, which, they term as intuition. Everything can be explained by science, as my Chinese colleague says, and everything that cannot be will be in future with the advancement of science. The very definition of Science according to Merriam Webster dictionary - the state of knowing: knowledge as distinguished from ignorance or misunderstanding.

In the same book of his, Paulo Coelho, in the chapter “The Warrior and Faith”, cites a legend where a disciple asked a priest in the monastery of Piedra, ‘Does prayer bring God closer to mankind?’ ‘I am going to reply with another question’ the priest said. ‘Will the prayers you say make the sun rise tomorrow?’ ‘Of course not! The sun rises in obedience to a universal law.’ ‘Well there is the answer to your question. God is closer to us regardless of how much we pray.’ The disciple was shocked. ‘Are you saying that our prayers are useless?’ ‘Absolutely not. If you do not wake up early enough, you will never get to see the sunrise. And although God is always close, if you do not pray, you will never manage to feel his presence.’

The Atheists have been eating His Cake, but without the cream. The cream that makes them feel Him. Feel Him around them. There is no such thing as, we don’t believe in God because there is nothing that can prove His existence, as they believe in, as they say, materialistic things. I am not saying they are wrong; instead, what I am saying is that they are missing the essence that keeps all their materialistic things going. But little they realise this void as they are constantly trying to find answers for the unanswerable. Coming back to Jerry, he tries to pull out a placard whenever someone asks or even praises him of his courageous outrage. The placard reads, “Love turns mountains.” From another chapter in his book Like the Flowing River, a brilliant collection of truth that we are all aware of but still somewhere have stopped contemplating I must say, Paulo Coelho, describes the work of another great writer Henry Drummond as follows:

Since earliest times people have asked the great question: what is the supreme good? You have life before you. You can live it only once. What is the noblest object of desire, the supreme gift to covet? We have been accustomed to be told that the greatest thing in the religious world is faith. That great word has been the keynote for centuries of the popular religion; and we have easily learned to look upon it as the greatest thing in the world. Well we are wrong. If we have been told that, we may miss the mark. In the 13th chapter of 1 Corinthians, Paul takes us to Christianity at its source, and there we see, ‘The greatest of these is love.’ It is not an oversight. Paul was speaking of faith just a moment before. He says, ‘And if I have all faith, so that I can remove mountains, and have not love, I am nothing.’ So far from forgetting, he deliberately contrasts them, “Now abideth faith, hope, love’, and without a moment’s hesitation the decision falls, ‘The greatest of these is love.’

So may be the Atheists and the so-called Non-religious people are leading their mundane lives because of the existence of Love in their lives. May be they have been doing their deeds which abstain them from immoral consequences. As rightly said by a great man,

God suffers from poor oversight, not because He’s old, but because that’s how He wants it to be. That way, when someone does something wrong, He can always say He couldn’t quite see, and so ends up forgiving the person because He does not want to commit an injustice. And when someone does something good; He, of course, never leaves His glasses at home!
